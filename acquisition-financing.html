Acquisition Financing

Commercial Acquisition Loan Broker

Debt and equity to acquire commercial real estate — structured for certainty of close, so you can compete and win on the deals that matter most.

What It Is

Capital to close the purchase.

Acquisition financing funds the purchase of income-producing or development-ready commercial real estate. In most large transactions it is a full capital stack — senior debt sized to the property's value or purchase price, often paired with mezzanine debt or equity to bridge the gap between the loan and the total cost. We assemble that stack so you can close with confidence.

When It Makes Sense

Built for the deals you can't afford to lose.

The right acquisition structure depends on what you're buying and why. A stabilized asset, a value-add play, and a portfolio purchase each call for a different mix of debt and equity — so we start with your business plan and your exit, then build the financing to match.

  • Purchasing a stabilized, income-producing asset
  • Acquiring a value-add property to reposition
  • Competing on a time-sensitive, off-market opportunity
  • Buying out a partner or acquiring a portfolio in one transaction
  • Closing a deal that needs more than senior debt alone
How We Structure It

The right stack for the asset and the business plan.

We source senior debt from banks, debt funds, and non-bank lenders, and layer in mezzanine or equity where the deal needs it — negotiating leverage, rate, term, recourse, and reserves around your return targets and your exit. Certainty of close is the priority: we package the deal so lenders can move, and we manage the process all the way to the closing table.

The Capital Stack

How an acquisition's capital comes together.

Most large acquisitions are funded by a capital stack, not a single loan. Senior acquisition debt from banks, debt funds, or non-bank lenders forms the base, sized to the property's value or purchase price. Where that falls short of what the deal requires, a mezzanine layer or equity fills the gap between the senior loan and total cost — including closing costs and any planned capital improvements.

As an illustration only, a stabilized acquisition might be financed largely with senior debt, while a value-add purchase often carries a lower senior loan plus mezzanine and sponsor equity to fund both the purchase and the repositioning. The exact mix depends on the asset, the business plan, and the market — and is never a quoted term.

Deal Types

The acquisitions we finance.

A stabilized acquisition of an income-producing asset is underwritten mostly on in-place cash flow and favors permanent or long-term debt. A value-add acquisition is underwritten on the business plan and often pairs a bridge loan with equity to fund both the purchase and the repositioning. Portfolio purchases and partner buyouts add their own structuring questions, which we work through as part of the capital plan.

Underwriting

What capital sources look for in an acquisition.

Acquisition lenders underwrite the asset you're buying and the plan you have for it. Expect close attention to:

  • The purchase price and your basis relative to the market
  • In-place net operating income and debt-service coverage
  • The business plan — especially rent or revenue growth on value-add deals
  • Your track record with the asset type and the market
  • Certainty and timing of close, which sellers weigh heavily
The Process

From LOI to the closing table.

An acquisition runs on a clock, and the financing has to keep pace with it. Once you're under a letter of intent or purchase agreement, we move quickly to a term sheet, then through lender due diligence on the asset and the business plan while the full capital stack comes together. The goal is a financing commitment that holds through diligence, so your offer carries real certainty of close.

After closing, the entry debt is matched to the plan: a stabilized buyer may already sit in long-term permanent debt, while a value-add buyer plans to stabilize the asset and then refinance into permanent financing. We structure the acquisition with that next step already in view.

Common Questions

Good to know.

How much of the purchase price can you finance?

It depends on the asset, its cash flow, and your business plan. Senior debt commonly covers a majority of value or cost; where you need more, we layer mezzanine or equity to complete the stack rather than forcing it all onto one loan.

Can you combine acquisition debt with equity in one deal?

Yes — most of our larger acquisitions are structured capital stacks. We coordinate senior lenders, mezzanine providers, and equity partners so the pieces fit together and close on the same timeline.

How fast can you close an acquisition?

Speed depends on the lender and the deal, but certainty of close is the priority. With a clean, well-packaged deal and the right capital sources lined up early, acquisition financing can move quickly — and we manage the process to the closing table so your offer's timeline holds.
