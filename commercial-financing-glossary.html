Commercial Financing Glossary

Commercial Financing Terms, Explained

A plain-English glossary of the debt, equity, and structuring terms that come up in large commercial real estate financing — from the capital stack to DSCR.

Acquisition Financing

Debt and often equity used to purchase an existing or development-ready commercial property. Learn more.

Amortization

The gradual repayment of a loan's principal over time through scheduled payments. A loan can be fully amortizing or carry a balloon payment at maturity.

Bridge Loan

Short-term financing, often 12–36 months, used to acquire, reposition, or stabilize a property before permanent financing or a sale. Learn more.

Capital Stack

The full combination of debt and equity used to fund a transaction, ordered by priority: senior debt, mezzanine, preferred equity, and common equity. Read the guide.

Cap Rate (Capitalization Rate)

A property's net operating income divided by its value, used to estimate return and value. A lower cap rate generally implies a higher value.

Common Equity

Ownership capital that is last in line for repayment and bears the most risk, but captures the residual upside if the project performs.

Construction Loan

Financing for ground-up development or major renovation, typically funded in draws as the project reaches defined milestones. Learn more.

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)

Cash flow available for debt service divided by required debt payments. A 1.25x DSCR means $1.25 of cash flow for every $1 of debt service; lenders use it to gauge repayment capacity.

Debt Yield

A lender's net operating income divided by the loan amount, used to measure risk independent of interest rate or amortization.

Equity Placement

Connecting a sponsor with investors who provide ownership capital for a project, rather than debt. Learn more.

Interest Reserve

Funds set aside within a construction or bridge loan to cover interest payments before the property generates income.

Loan-to-Cost (LTC)

The loan amount expressed as a percentage of total project cost; commonly used in construction financing.

Loan-to-Value (LTV)

The loan amount expressed as a percentage of the property's appraised value.

Mezzanine Financing

Subordinated debt that sits between senior debt and equity, filling the gap in the capital stack at a higher cost. Read the guide.

Net Operating Income (NOI)

A property's income after operating expenses but before debt service and taxes; the basis for most commercial real estate valuation.

Permanent Financing

Long-term, often fixed-rate debt that replaces construction or bridge financing once a property is stabilized.

Preferred Equity

An equity investment with negotiated priority over common equity for distributions, sometimes functioning similarly to subordinated debt.

Recapitalization

Restructuring a property's capital stack — debt, equity, or both — to pull out equity, retire maturing debt, or change ownership. Learn more.

Recourse / Non-Recourse

Whether a lender can pursue a borrower's other assets beyond the collateral if the loan defaults. Non-recourse limits the lender to the property itself.

Refinance

Replacing existing debt with new financing, often to lower cost, extend the term, or access built-up equity. Learn more.

Senior Debt

The largest, lowest-cost, highest-priority layer of the capital stack, typically secured by a first mortgage. Read the guide.

Takeout Financing

The permanent loan or sale that repays (takes out) a construction or bridge loan at maturity.

Term Sheet

A non-binding outline of proposed financing terms — amount, rate, term, fees, and conditions — issued before full documentation.
