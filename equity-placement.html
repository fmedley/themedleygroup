Equity Placement

Commercial Real Estate Equity Placement

We connect sponsors and developers with the equity partners who fit the deal — JV equity, preferred equity, and institutional capital for large commercial projects.

What It Is

The right capital partner for the deal.

When debt alone won't fund a project, you need equity. Equity placement is the process of matching your deal with the right capital partners — joint-venture equity, preferred equity, or limited-partner capital — on terms that protect your economics and your control. For a developer or sponsor, the partner you choose shapes not just whether a deal closes, but how profits are split, who holds decision rights, and what happens if the business plan shifts.

Equity is the most expensive layer of capital, because the investors who provide it take the most risk and expect the highest return. A well-run placement isn't simply about finding money — it's about finding capital that is priced fairly, structured to fit your plan, and committed for the life of the project.

Where It Sits

Equity in the capital stack.

Every commercial transaction is funded by a capital stack — the layered combination of debt and equity that together cover the total cost. Senior debt typically fills the largest, lowest-cost portion. Mezzanine financing or preferred equity can bridge the middle. Common equity sits at the bottom: last to be repaid, first to absorb losses, and entitled to the residual upside.

Because equity carries the most risk, sponsors generally contribute as little common equity as a deal allows and raise the rest from outside partners. Equity placement is how that outside capital is sourced and structured — and where it sits relative to the debt determines its cost, its control rights, and its return.

Structures

Common equity structures.

JV equityPreferred equityLP equityCo-GP equityProgrammatic equity

Joint-venture (JV) equity brings a partner alongside you who shares in both the upside and the major decisions. Preferred equity sits ahead of common equity, taking a fixed, priority return before the sponsor participates — closer in behavior to mezzanine debt than to ownership. Limited-partner (LP) equity is more passive capital that backs your business plan while leaving day-to-day control with you.

The right structure depends on how much control you want to keep, how the returns should be split, and how much risk each side is willing to carry. Part of our job is helping you weigh those trade-offs before you ever sit across from an investor.

When You Need It

Beyond your own balance sheet.

  • To fill the equity portion of a capital stack when debt stops short
  • To scale beyond what your own balance sheet can carry
  • To bring an institutional partner into a larger or more complex development
  • To fund the gap between a construction loan and total project cost
  • To recapitalize an existing asset and return capital to current investors
How We Work

Packaged, positioned, and negotiated.

We package your opportunity into the materials institutional investors expect to see, position it to the equity sources whose mandates fit the asset type and market, and help you negotiate the terms that matter most — the promote, the preferred return, capital-call provisions, and the control and approval rights that govern the partnership.

Our capital advisory role is to run a disciplined process, so you're comparing real, competing terms rather than taking the first offer — and so the partnership is built to work for the long term, not just at closing.

Underwriting

What equity partners look at.

Equity investors underwrite the sponsor as closely as the asset. Expect a serious partner to examine:

  • Your track record and experience with this asset type
  • The business plan, the projected returns, and the assumptions behind them
  • The market, your basis, and the downside if revenue disappoints
  • How much common equity you're contributing alongside them
  • The exit — sale or refinancing — and how and when capital is returned

Common Questions

Good to know.

What's the difference between JV and preferred equity?

JV equity shares in the upside and in control; preferred equity takes a fixed, priority return ahead of common equity. We'll help you weigh which structure fits your goals.

How is equity different from a loan?

A lender is repaid a fixed amount on a schedule and has no claim on the upside. An equity partner contributes capital in exchange for a share of the returns and, often, a voice in major decisions. Our guide to equity financing explains the trade-offs in more depth.

Do you place debt and equity together?

Yes. Many projects need both, and the layers have to be structured to work together. We can arrange the full capital stack — senior debt, mezzanine, and equity — so the pieces fit rather than conflict.

How large are the equity placements you arrange?

We work on large commercial transactions, with total capitalizations ranging from $10 million to $400 million and beyond.
